# Demo fuel — pre-vetted pieces for the "I'll bring something" door

When the opening question surfaces nothing — no unread tab, no newsletter, nothing the user made — the AI brings the fuel, and bringing something *genuinely good* is itself a small flex. These pieces are pre-vetted for the job: broad appeal to operators/creators/consultants, dense with extractable insight (they shine when os-gold chews them), freely accessible, and text-acquirable. Verified free-and-loading 2026-07-18, extraction-proven entries re-verified 2026-08-01; links rot, so if one fails, say so plainly and use the next in its slot — never let a dead link stall the demo.

Offer **two** that fit the person (never the whole menu), pitched by what's in it for them, not by format. And pitch the *outcome*, not the reading: the demo isn't "here's a good video," it's "hand me this and you walk away with something you keep." Three outcome shapes to offer from, matched to the person's appetite in the moment:

- **The best ideas, extracted** — a save-worthy insight list (`os-gold`). Lightest, fastest, always works.
- **A content kit** — angles, questions, and quotable lines ready to become posts and emails (`os-content-mining`, atomize). For people who publish.
- **A step-by-step playbook** — the how-to pulled out as a procedure they can follow this week (`os-content-mining`, sop mode). The strongest wow for operators; several pieces below have been run through this end-to-end, and the per-piece notes say what each yields.

## Picking for the person

Read what the hook told you: a coach leans Taki; a writer/creator leans Koe; a founder or generalist leans Hormozi or PG; someone tactical leans Superhuman; a freelancer, designer, or agency person leans Enns or Do. The piece is the demo's raw material — its *appeal to this person* matters more than its objective quality ranking.

## When fetching fights back

Users arrive primed for Claude Code, but harnesses and networks vary — a piece that fetched cleanly at verification time can 403 today. During the demo, don't fight it: per the paste-back shortcut in `../../_shared/references/web-content-acquisition.md`, one clean attempt per rung, then hand the user the URL and ask them to paste the text back. Thirty seconds of their help beats five minutes of visible workarounds — and framed plainly ("fastest way through: open this and paste me what's there"), it reads as teamwork, not failure. The Buzzsprout podcast is the most fetch-robust piece on this list (transcript inline, plain page load); lean toward it when the harness already feels thin.
