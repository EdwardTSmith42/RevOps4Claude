# Simon Bowen's Models Method — Reference

Domain reference for `modes/models-method`. A comprehensive prose summary of Simon Bowen's Models Method — a visual-communication methodology for consultants, coaches, and course creators who want to eliminate price-based sales conversations through structured visual frameworks.

## Core premise

Bowen's foundational claim:

> "If you say something to somebody, it's easily ignored. If you show something to somebody, it becomes interesting. But if you put structure to that thing that you show them, it becomes unforgettable."

Three levels: **Say → Show → Structured Show.** The Models Method lives at the third level. Every model is a structured visual that prospects can see, trust, and use to evaluate future alternatives.

The methodology transforms "intuitive genius" (what the practitioner does well but can't explain) into "organized genius" (visual frameworks that capture the thinking behind the work). Organized genius scales. Intuitive genius doesn't.

## Why it works (psychology)

- **Dual-process decision-making.** Type 1 (fast, intuitive) and Type 2 (slow, analytical). Visual models engage Type 1 first (pattern recognition), then give Type 2 structure to evaluate. Both layers activated, cognitive load stays low.
- **Visual Superiority Effect.** Visual messages are processed substantially faster than text and are more likely to be shared and retained.
- **Framework dependency.** Once prospects internalize a visual model, they use it as the standard for evaluating all future alternatives. Competitors must then compete against your framework rather than on their own strengths.
- **Pattern interruption.** Well-designed models "rattle paradigms" — they don't merely transmit information. They expand the prospect's mental framework and create buyer safety from the structure itself.
- **Mirror neuron activation.** When practitioners draw models in real-time (rather than showing completed slides), prospects' mirror neuron systems may activate, creating deeper emotional engagement with the success scenarios.

These effects compound: pattern interruption gets attention, visual superiority makes the model memorable, framework dependency makes it durable, dual-process activation closes the decision.

## The flagship: Genius Model

The **Genius Model** captures the practitioner's unique value proposition in a visual framework walkable in 7–11 minutes. It's the strategic bridge between vision, mission, and delivery — transforming business complexity into a "sight-bite."

**Structure:** positions the client's needs at the center, surrounded by the business's unique genius and methodology. Creates an immediate visual connection between customer problems and proprietary solutions.

**Choreography:** most effective when drawn live in real-time during client conversations, not pre-rendered in slides. Real-time drawing creates "organic engagement that draws people in" (both literally and figuratively). The 7–11-minute window matters — shorter and the model lacks depth, longer and attention drifts.

Practitioners consistently report that the Genius Model eliminates commodity-based competition because prospects can see the distinctive thinking and structured approach that separates the business from generic service providers.

## Supporting models

### Four Futures Model
Visually demonstrates the compound effects of decisions over time — the divergent outcomes of action vs. inaction. Typically a quadrant (2×2) contrasting choices × time. Strong in strategy sessions where clients need to visualize long-term consequences and commit to direction. Paired with the Genius Model in client conversations, practitioners report high conversion rates.

### Iceberg Model
Three-level depth structure for elevating conversations from transactional to transformational.

- **Tip (Transactional):** surface-level problems the prospect initially presents
- **Middle (Transformational):** deeper emotional and aspirational drivers that create real buying motivation
- **Foundation (Timeless Impact):** profound, long-lasting changes that justify premium pricing and create deep client relationships

Bowen's illustrative example: *"Do you want to treat your acne?"* (transactional) vs. *"Do you want skin you love living in?"* (transformational). The three-level progression systematically elevates price-focused conversations into value-based partnerships.

### "Now, Where, How" Tension Model
Creates productive tension in sales conversations, particularly for stage presentations and large-group conversions. Features Bowen's signature line:

> *"I'm not looking for Below the Line people looking for Above the Line results. I'm looking for Above the Line people getting Below the Line results."*

This statement simultaneously filters prospects (disqualifies price-focused buyers) and creates desire to be positioned "above the line." The model works by acknowledging the gap between where prospects are and where they want to be, positioning the presenter as the bridge.

### Above the Line vs. Below the Line Model
Related to Now-Where-How. Visually demonstrates different client types and positions premium services "above the line" where serious buyers operate. Particularly effective in B2B for filtering multi-stakeholder decisions toward quality prospects.

### Context-Concept-Content Framework
The methodological backbone for developing models.

- **Context:** deep understanding of the customer's current situation and future needs
- **Concept:** the strategic approach or solution framework
- **Content:** specific tactical and operational elements of delivery

Practitioners fluidly move between these three to catalyze success, ensuring models stay grounded in customer reality while delivering strategic value through tactical execution.

## Geometric principles

Different geometric shapes carry different cognitive meanings. Choose the shape to match the relationship being communicated.

| Shape | Use for |
|---|---|
| **Triangle** | Holistic dynamics — all three elements are required for success (remove one, the whole fails) |
| **Circle** | Continuous loops, ongoing processes, cyclical dynamics |
| **Venn diagram** | Overlapping concepts — intersections show where value lives |
| **2×2 Matrix** | Comparative dynamics, positioning, four-quadrant analysis |
| **Straight Line** | Continuums, before-and-after comparisons, progressive stages |

The choice is semantic, not decorative. A model using the wrong shape feels subtly off even when the content is right.

## Three model types (for building)

When helping a user create their own model, determine which type their situation needs:

- **Genius Model** — for users who struggle to explain their offer clearly. Goal: capture organizational genius in a 7–11 minute visual. Often uses triangle, Venn, or concentric circles.
- **Conversion Model** — for users who struggle to get leads to convert. Goal: install a belief shift or decision frame in the prospect. Often uses quadrants (Four Futures-style) or arcs / before-after contrasts.
- **Clarity Model** — for users whose clients are confused about their own situation. Goal: help prospects see where they are and what they need. Often uses quadrants, spectrums, or diagnostic scorecards.

Listen for what the user needs rather than forcing a type.

## Implementation — four phases

The full Models Method rollout for a business is a four-phase process:

**Phase 1 — Discovery and Foundation.** Deep-dive sessions to identify business genius and extract context-driven insights. Practitioners go "two levels down" from surface service descriptions to the profound wisdom competitors miss.

**Phase 2 — Model Development.** Create the suite of visual models driving client attraction and conversion. Start with the flagship Genius Model. Add supporting models for objection handling, value demonstration, urgency, and differentiation. Each model follows the geometric principles above.

**Phase 3 — Sales Integration.** Transform models from presentation tools into systematic selling. Train practitioners and teams on model-based conversation, develop the choreography, create scalable sales systems that don't depend on individual talent.

**Phase 4 — Delivery and Implementation.** Extend model usage beyond sales into service delivery, client onboarding, team alignment, and ongoing communication. Models become the foundation for consistent messaging across every customer touchpoint.

The `modes/models-method` mode typically focuses on Phase 2 — helping a user develop a specific model for their business. Phases 1, 3, and 4 are implementation work that sits outside the skill's scope.

## Green Zone vs. Red Zone positioning

Strategic positioning layer: practitioners operate in either the **Green Zone** (elevated positioning, premium pricing, deep client relationships, category-of-one) or the **Red Zone** (commodity competition, price comparison, interchangeable vendors). Models are the primary mechanism for moving from Red Zone to Green Zone.

Three positioning shifts required:

1. **Ego-based → Wisdom-based selling.** Organized genius does the talking, not pushy sales technique.
2. **Pressure-based → Presence-based influence.** Make buying the safest decision for the customer rather than creating artificial urgency.
3. **Complex communication → Clear communication.** Package complexity into simple parcels of clarity that prospects can immediately understand and trust.

The Green Zone position is sometimes called "sage positioning" — accepting and normalizing customer chaos, positioning yourself as the simplifier who brings calm. Prospects seek guidance rather than defending against sales pressure. Power dynamic shifts from vendor-prospect to wise-advisor-seeking-client.

## Constraints — what not to do

These are the mode's hard constraints, each preserved from source.

- **3–4 pillars max.** More pillars dilute memorability. Cognitive-load ceiling.
- **Avoid buzzwords and vagueness.** Use language that makes sense to outsiders. The model should work for a smart 14-year-old.
- **Core logic before visuals.** Don't move to shape/sketch until the underlying insight is sound. A beautifully-drawn bad model is worse than a rough sketch of a good one.
- **Don't force a model type.** Listen for what the user's situation actually needs (Genius / Conversion / Clarity). A forced type produces a flat model.
- **Don't draw for the user.** The user sketches and verbalizes. The mode guides. Ownership matters — a model someone else drew is never as sticky as one you drew yourself.
- **7th-grade readability.** The whole premise of the method is visual + simple beats complex + text-heavy. Jargon-laden output violates the method.
