# Positioning Blueprint — canonical output shape

Use this shape for `modes/positioning`. Six-section output produced AFTER the interview completes. Each section serves a different downstream use (elevator pitch, landing page, one-pager, ad copy).

## Shape

```markdown
# Positioning Blueprint: [User's Name or Business Name]

## 1. Elevator Pitch

A single sentence the user could say at a networking event without sounding rehearsed. Names *who* they help, *what change* the help produces, and *why that change matters to that audience*. Avoid the generic "I help X with Y so they can Z" mold unless that mold genuinely fits the user's voice — invented from-scratch phrasing in the user's own register beats the template every time.

*Optional extensions (use only when they land naturally and the user would actually say them):*
- An extension that captures the *unique mechanism* — the vehicle behind the pitch, described concretely (named only if a real name already exists).
- An extension that names the *feared cost or failed-alternative* they're working around — useful when the audience is jaded by prior attempts.

---

## 2. Value Breakdown

**Target Avatar:**
[Who they work with, in context — specific niche, named characteristics, situational markers]

**Core Outcomes:**
- *Tangible:* [specific observable changes — metrics, artifacts, capabilities]
- *Intangible:* [emotional / identity / relational changes]

**Zone of Genius:**
[What they do best and love to do — often where energy rises in the interview]

**Non-Genius Work:**
[What they refuse to do, even if capable — the "I don't" clauses that signal discipline]

**Process / Philosophy:**
[Any repeatable IP, methodology, or belief set that shapes how they work]

**Why It Matters:**
[The larger "so what" — why their work matters beyond the immediate service]

**Proof Pattern:**
[The type of results they consistently deliver — the shape of client transformations]

---

## 3. USP Snapshot

[A single paragraph combining the four USP components:]

1. *Unique belief or philosophy* — what they believe about the domain that most competitors don't
2. *Distinct approach or process* — what they do differently in the work
3. *Unmatched promise or experience* — the specific outcome they stand behind
4. *Unlike Statement* — a sentence that names a *specific competitive pattern the prospect has likely encountered* and the distinct way the user's work breaks from it. "Unlike most coaches" is weak. A specific named alternative pattern (the thing the buyer has actually tried and been burned by) is strong.

---

## 4. NTPV Messaging Blueprint

- **Niche:** [who you serve, with specificity — not "coaches" but "solopreneur coaches stuck at $10K MRR"]
- **Transformation:** [what they become — identity-level or capability-level, not just "have X"]
- **Promise:** [the specific outcome you stand behind]
- **Vehicle:** [how you deliver the transformation — the mechanism or methodology, described concretely enough that a stranger could tell it apart from a competitor's; add the name only if one exists]

---

## 5. Value Articulation Story (7-Beat Format)

**Most** [target audience] [current struggle or approach]...

**And that's a problem because** [why the current approach fails]...

**Because of that,** [negative consequences]...

**Which means** [emotional or practical impact]...

**On the other hand,** [your different approach]...

**So that** [immediate benefits]...

**And consequently,** [ultimate transformation]...

---

## 6. Top 5 Objections and Responses

1. **"[Stated objection in the prospect's voice]"**
   *Reframe:* [how to dissolve the objection — not argue against, reframe]

2. **"[Stated objection]"**
   *Reframe:* [...]

3. **"[Stated objection]"**
   *Reframe:* [...]

4. **"[Stated objection]"**
   *Reframe:* [...]

5. **"[Stated objection]"**
   *Reframe:* [...]

---

## Closing Invitation

A short, warm one- or two-sentence invitation that names the blueprint as a *foundation* (not a final), and asks the user whether any part doesn't quite capture the essence of their work — with an offer to revise. Write it in the same voice as the rest of the blueprint.
```

## Key rules

- **Use the user's exact phrasing wherever possible.** Strip analyst-polish, preserve voice. This is the main craft of the mode. A positioning blueprint written in the user's words is usable. One written in generic-business-speak isn't.
- **Elevator Pitch must feel natural to say aloud.** If it only works on paper, rewrite. The user should be able to say this in a real-world conversation.
- **Niche is specific.** "Coaches" fails. "Solopreneur coaches stuck at $10K MRR for 18+ months" passes. The narrower the niche, the sharper everything downstream.
- **Transformation names identity, not just outcome.** "Makes $30K/month" is an outcome. "Runs a leveraged business" is a transformation. Identity-level transformations are more durable positioning.
- **Vehicle is described, not just labeled.** "Coaching" is generic; "a three-phase productization system that moves a service business from custom work to a sellable offer" is a vehicle. Description is what matters — most businesses haven't named their vehicle yet, and that's not a gap. If the operator wants to productize it later, a name can come then; early names are internal-only working titles, never forced during the interview.
- **Unlike Statement names a specific alternative.** "Unlike most coaches" is weak. "Unlike content-heavy courses that leave you stalled at module 3" is strong and buyer-recognizable.
- **Value Articulation Story hits all 7 beats.** Skipping beats produces uneven narrative pressure. Completing all 7 is the discipline.
- **Objections are in the prospect's voice.** First-person, not analyst-paraphrased. These are *anticipated* objections — drafted by the AI from the interview material plus knowledge of how prospects in this market actually hesitate. That's the section's job: most operators don't think about objections at all, or not early enough, so the blueprint puts strong ones in front of them whether or not the interview surfaced any. When the operator did report real prospect language, prefer it verbatim. Each reframe dissolves the objection rather than arguing against it.
- **Closing invitation is in voice, not boilerplate.** Positioning is iterative; the invitation to revise is part of the deliverable. Write it in the same register as the blueprint, not as a template footer.
- **Cross-mode suggestions live outside this artifact.** Anything pasteable — Elevator Pitch, USP, NTPV — will get copied into other surfaces. The blueprint ends at the closing invitation; cross-mode suggestions sit after it, in a separate block, never stitched in.

## Grounding check before shipping

1. Does the Elevator Pitch pass the "say it out loud at a networking event" test?
2. Is the Niche specific enough that a reader could identify or reject themselves as belonging to it?
3. Does the Transformation name an identity shift, not just a circumstantial outcome?
4. Does the Unlike Statement name a specific alternative pattern the prospect has likely encountered?
5. Is every section in the user's voice, with minimal generic business language?
