# Mode: positioning

## Purpose

Surface a user's positioning through a structured interview — one question at a time, drawn from a menu of question shapes — and produce a complete positioning blueprint in their own language. The output includes six shapes (Elevator Pitch, Value Breakdown, USP Snapshot, NTPV Messaging Blueprint, Value Articulation Story, Top 5 Objections) — each serving a different downstream use (elevator pitch, landing page, one-pager, ad copy, sales call).

## Triggers

Phrases like: *"help me find my positioning,"* *"positioning blueprint,"* *"clarify my positioning,"* *"what should I say about my business,"* *"elevator pitch,"* *"USP,"* *"NTPV,"* *"position my business,"* *"positioning interview,"* *"what's my unique value."*

Also triggers when upstream audience / market work has been done and the user is ready to turn it into positioning language.

## Inputs

See `references/market-input-context.md`. Positioning is unusual among biz-strategy modes — it doesn't need pre-supplied context. The mode is an interview, and inputs come from the user's answers as the conversation unfolds.

- **Not required upfront:** A business description or audience segment. The opening question invites reflection, and context builds from the user's answers.
- **Optional:** Prior positioning attempts, avatar output, market map. Useful context but the interview works without them.

## Run

**Step 1 — Open the interview.** Open with a *broad, invitational* question about the kind of business the user is in — phrased in a way that invites reflection rather than diagnosis. A diagnostic opener (*"What's your business model?"*) produces a short answer. An invitational opener produces a paragraph that reveals how the user thinks about themselves, which is the first piece of positioning material. Keep the opener general and unhurried; specificity comes later.

**Step 2 — Conduct the interview.** One question at a time, drawn from the menu of shapes below. Adaptive follow-ups based on the user's answers. Conversational, collaborative, exploratory tone — not transactional.

The questions should collectively surface:
- Target audience and niche context
- Repeating client challenges and the outcomes they pay for
- Zone of genius (what only they can do)
- Transformation they create
- Unique approach or personality edge, and the process edge — how they do what they do
- Dislikes, boundaries, and non-negotiables
- Long-term purpose or deeper motivation
- The elements of their USP
- Their NTPV (Niche / Transformation / Promise / Vehicle)

Design each question to do one of the following kinds of work. Treat the list as a *menu of question shapes*, not a script — the exact wording is yours to choose for the user in front of you. Mix shapes; don't ask three of the same kind in a row.

- An *origin question* that surfaces why they got into the work — exposes mission and motivation
- A *gratitude question* about what clients consistently thank them for — surfaces unstated value
- A *misrepresentation question* about what they'd correct first if their work were described wrong — surfaces what they consider essential to who they are
- A *flow question* about when they're most energized in the work — locates the Zone of Genius
- An *invisible-problem question* about what they fix that the client didn't know was broken — surfaces tacit expertise
- A *referral-language question* about what favorite clients say when they refer the user — captures language that already works in-market
- A *frustration question* about what bothers them most about how others in their field work — surfaces philosophy through contrast
- A *not-on-the-website question* about what they do that doesn't fit neatly in marketing copy — surfaces depth that didn't make it across
- A *belief question* about a working principle they hold that many in the field overlook — surfaces positioning-worthy convictions
- A *one-promise question* about the single outcome they'd stand behind if forced to pick — forces the NTPV Promise
- A *story question* asking for a success that captures the essence of the work — surfaces proof and pattern in one move
- A *failed-alternative question* about what clients typically try before them and why it doesn't work — sets up the Unlike Statement
- A *tipping-point question* about what would tip a choice between them and a close alternative — surfaces the differentiator

Each shape probes a different angle on positioning material. Follow up on any answer that signals something deeper; the follow-up is often where the real positioning lives. Present exactly one question at a time. Wait for the answer before advancing.

**Step 3 — Silent observation throughout.** Internally track (do NOT reveal during the interview):

- Language patterns — recurring phrases, metaphors, emotional registers
- Zone of Genius spikes — where the user's energy rises
- Fuzzy vs. clear articulation — which parts of positioning they've worked out vs. still figuring out
- Tangible vs. intangible transformations they deliver
- Emotional drivers and philosophical through-lines
- Belief statements that could serve as positioning
- Precise audience markers and use-case signals
- Tension points and values that create contrast
- USP indicators: uncommon offers, traits, or beliefs
- NTPV structure as it surfaces
- Success patterns and proof points
- Common objections or misconceptions they face

See `references/positioning-concepts.md` for the full silent-observation protocol.

Revealing the tracking mid-interview causes the user to perform for the tracking rather than speaking honestly. Silence produces better material.

**Step 4 — Generate the positioning blueprint.** When the interview has surfaced the material above, produce the complete blueprint per `templates/positioning-output.md`. Six sections:

1. Elevator Pitch (1 sentence, natural to say aloud)
2. Value Breakdown (Target Avatar, Core Outcomes, Zone of Genius, Non-Genius Work, Process/Philosophy, Why It Matters, Proof Pattern)
3. USP Snapshot (paragraph with Unlike Statement)
4. NTPV Messaging Blueprint (Niche, Transformation, Promise, Vehicle)
5. Value Articulation Story (7-beat format: Most / And that's a problem because / Because of that / Which means / On the other hand / So that / And consequently)
6. Top 5 Objections and Responses

Use the user's exact phrasing wherever possible. Polish with analyst-speak loses the voice they'll actually use.

**Step 5 — Invite revision.** Close with a *short, warm* invitation to revise — name that this is a foundation rather than a final, and ask whether any part doesn't quite capture the essence of the user's work. Positioning is iterative; the invitation to revise is part of the deliverable, not boilerplate after it.

See `templates/positioning-output.md` for the canonical output shape.

## Output

Per `templates/positioning-output.md`. All six sections required. In the user's voice.

The positioning blueprint is a *pasteable strategic artifact* — the user will copy individual sections (Elevator Pitch, USP, NTPV) into other tools, drafts, and decks. Cross-mode suggestions must therefore live in a separate trailing block *after* the blueprint ends, never inside it or stitched onto its final line — otherwise the cross-mode prose gets pasted alongside positioning copy and contaminates downstream work.

After the blueprint and its closing invitation, include a brief cross-mode suggestion block — one or two adjacent modes that would sharpen the work, described by effect. Common next moves from a positioning blueprint: validating against the market landscape (`market-map`), turning the positioning into a memorable visual framework (`models-method`), or pressure-testing against strategic alternatives with an Inverse Thinking overlay (`blue-ocean`).

## Design Rationale

- **A menu of shapes, one question at a time** — conversational rhythm matters. The menu is order-agnostic and unnumbered on purpose: a fixed numbered questionnaire produces survey answers and interrogation feel, while one-at-a-time with adaptive follow-ups surfaces tacit material the user couldn't articulate in a form. (Counts in prose drift as menus evolve — describe the structure, don't number it. [elicited 2026-07-19])
- **Mirror their language** — the final blueprint uses the user's exact phrasing. Polishing with generic business-speak defeats the whole exercise; a blueprint they won't naturally speak is useless.
- **Silent observation without revealing** — if the analyst shows their work mid-interview, the user performs for the tracking. Silence produces better material.
- **Structured output with six different shapes** — each shape serves a different downstream use (Elevator Pitch for bio, Value Articulation Story for landing page, NTPV for one-pager, USP Snapshot for sales page, Objections for sales calls). Generating all six simultaneously, from the same interview material, is the high-value move.
- **Invitation-style opener** — diagnostic openers produce short answers. Invitations produce paragraphs that reveal how the user thinks about themselves. The opener is doing material-generation work, not throat-clearing.
- **Iterative closing invitation** — positioning is not final. The mode explicitly invites revision; a positioning blueprint is a starting point, not an endpoint.
- **Pasteable artifact discipline** — the blueprint will be copied section-by-section into other surfaces. Cross-mode suggestions sit outside the artifact, never inside it.
