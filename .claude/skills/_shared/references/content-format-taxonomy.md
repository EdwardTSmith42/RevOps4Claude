# Content Format Taxonomy

**This set can grow. The *kind* of cut is the canonical part; the exact count is not — read this file rather than restating its contents elsewhere.**

Six content formats that cover the main modes of short-form content for creators, coaches, and experts building authority online. Used when a skill needs to classify a content idea by its delivery form — or to generate one idea per format from a source.

Each format is a delivery mode, not a topic. The same underlying idea can be cast across multiple formats and land differently in each.

## The six formats

### 1. Practical how-to

Step-by-step breakdowns of how to do something specific. Focus on tactical, teachable execution that feels achievable.

**Strong when:** the steps are under-discussed, the problem is uncommon, or the hack has been overlooked. Generic how-tos are dead on arrival.

**Test:** could a reader follow these steps tonight and get a result tomorrow?

### 2. Strategic how-to

An opinionated, big-picture take on how to approach a familiar challenge in a dramatically more effective way. Less about steps, more about framework, principle, or unconventional mindset.

**Strong when:** the angle challenges a default approach and earns the challenge with reasoning.

**Test:** does this change *how* the reader thinks about the problem, not just *what* they do?

### 3. Lessons listicle

A concise set of transformational lessons tied to a single theme, experience, or journey. Feels earned — drawn from patterns observed, mistakes made, or systems built.

**Strong when:** the lessons are specific enough to be memorable and the theme is unified enough that they cohere.

**Test:** do all the lessons belong to the same story, or is this a grab-bag of unrelated tips pretending to be a listicle?

### 4. Thought leadership

A unique lens on trends, debates, or default ways of thinking in a field. Showcases original insight that sparks conversation, redefines the frame, or makes an uncomfortably honest observation.

**Strong when:** the insight is one the field hasn't yet articulated, and the writer has enough standing to articulate it without needing to justify standing.

**Test:** does the thought leader take a position the reader hasn't heard from three other accounts this week?

### 5. Rant

A sharp, energetic expression of frustration or dissatisfaction with a norm, tool, mindset, or behavior.

**Strong when:** the rant resonates with a niche-but-passionate tribe of readers who feel the same. Bonus for calling out a specific target (practice, trend, or behavior — not usually a person).

**Test:** would a reader forward this to a friend with "finally someone said it"?

### 6. Advice

Candid, experience-driven guidance. Advice the writer wishes someone had told them earlier. Personal, clear, brutally useful — not vague inspiration, but what actually works.

**Strong when:** rooted in a concrete situation the reader will recognize, with a specific counter-intuitive move.

**Test:** could a reader recount this advice in their own words tomorrow, and would it change what they do?

## How these differ from each other

- **Practical vs. Strategic how-to:** steps vs. frameworks. Practical is "what to do." Strategic is "how to think about what to do."
- **Lessons listicle vs. Thought leadership:** personal patterns vs. public take. Lessons is "here's what I learned." Thought leadership is "here's what the field is wrong about."
- **Rant vs. Advice:** emotional discharge vs. considered guidance. Rants run hot; advice runs warm-and-concrete.
- **Advice vs. Practical how-to:** advice is opinionated and situational; how-to is procedural.

## Using the taxonomy

When a skill needs to generate content ideas from a source:
- Generate at least one idea per format (forces breadth across delivery modes)
- Tag each idea with its format, explicitly
- Check that the idea actually fits the format (a practical how-to without steps isn't a practical how-to)

When a skill needs to classify an existing post:
- Match to the closest format
- Note if the post spans multiple (often a sign the post is trying to do too much)

## Malleability note

This taxonomy is reference-grade but malleable. The six categories capture a deliberate cut at short-form content delivery modes, refined through use — the *kind* of lens matters more than the exact count. A skill with a genuine need for a different cut (seven formats, a merged how-to category, a split on rant-vs-vent) can extend or prune. When a skill diverges meaningfully, note the reason and consider whether the modification reveals a gap this reference should absorb.
