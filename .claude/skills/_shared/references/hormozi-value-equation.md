# Hormozi's Value Equation

Alex Hormozi (*$100M Offers*). The prospect's perceived value of an offer is shaped by four variables:

> **Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)**

Every offer move either inflates the numerator or shrinks the denominator. The equation frames what offer articulation needs to cover:

- **Dream Outcome** — what the prospect ultimately wants. Specific, vivid, named. Weak dream outcomes produce weak offers; vague dreams don't motivate action.
- **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement** — whether the prospect believes they can actually get the outcome. Addressed through proof, credibility, similarity (prospects like them who got it), and mechanism clarity.
- **Time Delay** — how long until results arrive. Minimize when possible; reframe when not (shift the frame from "time to finish" to "time to first meaningful shift").
- **Effort & Sacrifice** — what the prospect has to give up or do. Minimize through done-for-you elements, templates, systems; reframe ("the effort is the feature — this is how you build the skill") when minimization isn't credible.

## Four levers, four questions

When reviewing or constructing an offer, work through all four:

| Lever | Question | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | Is the outcome specific and visceral, or generic and fuzzy? | Sharpen to the specific |
| Perceived Likelihood | Would a prospect believe this is possible for them? What blocks belief? | Increase through proof / credibility / similarity / mechanism |
| Time Delay | How long until results? Is that the actual bottleneck? | Minimize or reframe |
| Effort & Sacrifice | What does the prospect have to do? Is there a done-for-you or hybrid path? | Minimize or make the effort a feature |

A strong offer moves at least two levers well; moving all four is the grand-slam offer Hormozi writes about.

## Strong vs. weak articulation

| Lever | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | "More leads" | "12 qualified sales calls on your calendar within 30 days, from prospects who already raised their hand" |
| Perceived Likelihood | "Our system works" | "Here's how Sara (a 2-person agency just like yours) used it to book 9 calls in her first week; here's the specific step she did differently" |
| Time Delay | "Long-term growth" | "Your first qualified call within 14 days or we work for free until you get it" |
| Effort & Sacrifice | "Easy to implement" | "You spend 90 minutes onboarding; we run the system. Your ongoing time: under 2 hours/week reviewing calls our system booked for you." |

The pattern: generic abstractions score low on every lever. Specific, grounded, measurable claims score high.

## Malleability note

Reference-grade but malleable. The four levers (Dream Outcome / Likelihood / Time / Effort) are Hormozi canon and should not be renamed or reduced. What IS malleable:

- **Relative emphasis per context.** Some offers live or die on Time Delay (emergency or deadline-driven niches); others on Perceived Likelihood (skeptical/burned-before niches). Skills calibrate emphasis to the specific input rather than treating all four as equally weighted.
- **Depth of articulation.** In a quick offer sketch, one-liner per lever is fine. In a full offer vehicle spec, each lever gets a paragraph. Match depth to the work being done.
- **Whether to apply the equation literally vs. as a checklist.** Some skills use the equation as an analytical spine (walk each lever); others use it as a screen after articulation (check the output against all four). Both are valid — don't force a literal walk-through when a screen suffices.

Skills that diverge from literal application should note the reason in their Design Rationale.
