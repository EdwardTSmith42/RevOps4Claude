# Creative Criteria

**This set can grow. The *kind* of filter is the canonical part; the exact count is not — read this file rather than restating its contents elsewhere.**

Five criteria that describe ideas that actually travel — ones readers highlight, quote, forward, or act on. Used when a skill is generating, filtering, or evaluating content concepts, nuggets, or angles.

An idea doesn't need to hit all five. One is often enough to make an idea worth developing; two or three make it strong; rarely does a single item carry all five.

## The five criteria

### Counter-intuitive

Subverts expectations or goes against conventional wisdom in a way that feels insightful and earned.

**Strong counter-intuitive:** the obvious answer fails for a specific reason, and the non-obvious answer works for a reason the writer can articulate.

**Weak counter-intuitive:** contrarian for contrarianism's sake. "Everyone says X, but actually Y" without a reason Y holds up.

### Awe-inspiring

Evokes a sense of wonder, power, or possibility. Makes the reader say: "Whoa, I never thought of it that way."

**Strong awe:** a reframe or revelation that expands the reader's sense of what's possible in a domain.

**Weak awe:** hyperbolic marketing ("This ONE trick..."). Promise of awe without delivery.

### Elegantly simple

Distills a complex, messy concept into a crisp, clear, easily graspable idea. Bonus for metaphors, mental models, or vivid visuals.

**Strong elegance:** a phrase or model that collapses dozens of pages of expertise into a compact form that survives use.

**Weak elegance:** oversimplification that drops complexity the idea actually depends on. "Just do X" when the real answer is "depends on four conditions."

### Relatable

Even when tackling niche or advanced topics, the idea resonates with real challenges, real emotions, real people.

**Strong relatability:** the reader recognizes themselves in the setup, and the insight applies directly to their situation.

**Weak relatability:** generic-sounding appeals that could apply to anyone ("we all feel imposter syndrome sometimes"). Universal becomes unreadable.

### Actionable

The takeaway isn't just a thought — it leads to a shift in behavior, mindset, or approach. Every idea should spark motion.

**Strong actionable:** a specific next move the reader could take this week, with a clear condition when to apply it.

**Weak actionable:** vague calls to "reflect," "notice," or "be intentional about" something. Motion without direction.

## How to use the criteria

When generating content ideas:
- Aim for at least one criterion to be clearly satisfied per idea
- Prefer ideas where two or more criteria stack — they tend to travel farther

When filtering ideas:
- An idea hitting zero criteria is usually fluff — cut it
- An idea hitting one criterion strongly often outperforms an idea hitting three criteria weakly

When evaluating output from a content skill:
- If the output feels flat, check against the criteria — usually the missing element is Counter-intuitive or Actionable (those two are the most commonly dropped)
- If the output feels overworked, often Elegantly-simple has been violated

## Cross-reference: golden nugget criteria

A high-quality golden nugget typically hits one or more of these creative criteria AND the nugget-specific criteria (stand-alone, sticky, resonance). The overlap is substantial; use both when evaluating.

## Malleability note

Reference-grade but malleable. The five criteria capture the main pathways ideas travel (novelty, wonder, clarity, connection, utility), refined through use. The *kind* of filter matters more than the exact five; a skill with a genuine need for a sixth (e.g., "emotionally provocative") or a merge (e.g., "awe + counter-intuitive = surprise") can diverge. Note the reason when you do.
