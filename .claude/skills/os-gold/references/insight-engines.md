# Insight Engines

**This set can grow. The *kind* of triangulation is the canonical part; the exact count is not — read this file rather than restating its contents elsewhere.**

Three analytical engines applied in `os-gold/modes/deep-exploration.md`. Each engine is an orthogonal way to interrogate source material — together they produce depth that any single lens misses.

Apply each engine in sequence. Engines 1 and 2 operate on the content's logic and structure. Engine 3 operates on the mind that produced it.

## Engine 1: Recursive Question Architectures

Generate new questions, surface implicit assumptions, and interrogate surface answers.

### Question Cascades

- For every answer or claim in the text, generate 2–3 deeper questions it raises.
- Look for boundary points: where does the author's certainty end?

### Default Challenging

- Identify statements presented as obvious, natural, or universally true.
- Ask: *"What if this default were false? What would shift?"*
- The payoff is finding defaults the author never noticed they were relying on.

### Problem Behind the Solution

- Any time a solution is offered, reconstruct the problem it solves.
- Ask: *"What assumptions does this problem definition rely on?"*
- Solutions often expose a mental model in how they frame the problem.

## Engine 2: Analytical Skeleton Mapping

Expose the logical infrastructure of the content. Reduce narrative or example-heavy material to abstract structures.

### Argument Scaffolding

- Strip away stories, examples, and analogies.
- Rebuild core claims in terms of premises, logic, and conclusions.
- Identify logical leaps or non-sequiturs.

### Connection Mapping

- Map causal, enabling, or correlative relationships between concepts.
- Use arrows or structured bullets to show how ideas link.
- Makes dependencies visible — often reveals which claims are load-bearing for the whole argument.

## Engine 3: Authorial Cognition Tracing

Model the mental patterns and epistemic habits of the content creator.

### Rule Extraction

- Identify implicit operating principles ("Always do X when Y happens").
- Surface hidden decision trees or behavioral rules.
- The author may never state the rule. It shows up across their examples.

### Explanation Style Analysis

- Classify dominant explanatory styles: sequence, system, contrast, taxonomy.
- Ask: *what does this reveal about how the author structures knowledge?*
- A sequence-thinker treats everything as a timeline. A system-thinker sees feedback loops. A contrast-thinker defines by negation.

### Abstraction Ladder Tracking

- Track when the author moves up (to principles) or down (to details).
- Where they're confident, they tend to live at the specifics. Where they're vague or aspirational, they tend to live in the abstract.

### Repetition Significance

- Identify ideas stated multiple times in different words.
- These are almost always the author's core beliefs or priorities — the commitments the rest of the thinking rests on.

## Usage pattern

For a dense source, allocate attention roughly:
- Engine 1: scan the whole source for high-signal claims, generate 5–10 cascades
- Engine 2: pull the 2–3 key arguments into skeleton form
- Engine 3: identify the author's explanatory style and 1–3 repeated themes

Then the mode's final pass: extract golden nuggets that surface across both source *and* the exploration the engines produced.

## Malleability note

Reference-grade but malleable. The three engines are orthogonal axes for interrogating source (logic / structure / author-mind). The *kind* of triangulation matters more than the exact three. Skills with a genuine need for a fourth lens (emotional-register tracing, domain-context mapping) may extend. Note the reason when they do.
