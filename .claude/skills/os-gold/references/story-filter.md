# S.T.O.R.Y. Filter

A stickiness framework for converting a raw insight into a form that sticks in memory and compels action. Used in `os-gold/modes/video-transcript.md` for the "Make It Sticky" pass on the biggest extracted nugget. May also be useful to content-drafting and repurposing skills that want to sharpen a single insight.

## The five moves

Apply in order. Each move transforms the insight. The output of the last move should be noticeably more shareable than the input.

- **S — Simplify** until a 10-year-old gets it. Drop jargon, compress, metaphorize if needed. The goal isn't dumbing-down. It's removing the scaffolding between the insight and the reader.
- **T — Tell** it through a specific scene. Instead of stating the principle abstractly, put it inside a concrete moment with a place, a person, an action. ("The CEO walked into the office at 7am and saw..." not "Leaders who model early arrival tend to...")
- **O — Own** it with a personal angle. Attach the insight to a felt experience or POV. Without ownership the insight stays generic. With ownership it feels earned.
- **R — Relate** it to a universal truth. Connect the specific scene back to something the reader already believes or has experienced, so the insight lands as confirmation *and* expansion.
- **Y — Yield** a clear next action. End on a specific thing the reader could do differently, not a feeling they should sit with.

## What it's for

The S.T.O.R.Y. filter is applied to *the biggest insight*, not to every nugget. Running all five moves on every item would produce an exhausting output. One or two nuggets get the full treatment. The rest stay as clean extractions.

## What a transformed insight looks like

Take a raw nugget — a competent generalization about, say, advertising frequency or attention scarcity. After the five moves it should read as a short three-part artifact:

- An opening **scene** with a named person, a moment in time, and a specific action (Tell). The principle is buried inside the scene, not stated as a thesis.
- A middle **bridge** that connects the scene to the reader's own felt experience (Own + Relate) — a couple of sentences that name what the reader has already half-noticed in their own work, so the insight lands as confirmation expanding into something they hadn't articulated.
- A closing **single concrete action** (Yield) the reader could take this week — narrow enough to do, specific enough to know whether they did it.

The transformed version should be roughly the length of a short newsletter blurb. Longer than that is a sign the filter is being over-applied (see below). When sketching it out, do not pre-write phrasings the model will copy verbatim — let the scene, bridge, and action emerge from the actual nugget being transformed, not from a template's surface words.

## The failure mode

The output can read as overwritten if the S.T.O.R.Y. moves stack without restraint. Signs the filter is over-applied: every sentence has a scene, the personal ownership veers into self-indulgence, the closing action is vague ("notice something today") rather than specific ("repeat your core message one more time").

Rule: if the output wouldn't survive a skeptical reader's "so what?" test, trim rather than add.
