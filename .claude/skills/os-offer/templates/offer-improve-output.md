# Offer Improve — canonical output shape

Use this shape for `modes/offer-improve`. Six-section strategic review of an existing offer, grounded in direct-response psychology, pricing theory, and Blue Ocean positioning. Ends with 3-5 prioritized specific recommendations.

## Shape

```markdown
# Strategic Offer Review: [Offer Name / Description]

## 1. Offer Summary

[1-paragraph synthesis of the offer as understood. What's being sold, to whom, at what perceived value, what transformation is promised. If any element is unclear, name the ambiguity rather than glossing.]

---

## 2. Market Fit

- **Who is this really for?** [Tight audience description — if the stated audience is too broad, note that as a Market Fit issue]
- **What are they struggling with?** [Specific pain, grounded in the real prospect's situation, not the offer owner's assumption]
- **What outcome are they truly seeking?** [Emotional and functional outcomes — often different from what the offer surface-advertises]
- **Where does this offer under- or over-deliver?** [Honest read — specific gaps between what the audience needs and what the offer provides]

---

## 3. Value + Differentiation

- **What makes this unique or credible?** [The real differentiator, if it exists — or the gap, if it doesn't]
- **What frameworks, features, or outcomes could be better articulated?** [Specific articulation opportunities — often the offer has hidden value that's under-communicated]
- **What might prospects still be unsure about?** [Objections and gaps in the proof stack]
- **Does the offer have a named Unique Mechanism or Vehicle?** [If yes, is the name credible and non-generic? If no, flag as a differentiation opportunity — see `../references/unique-mechanism-playbook.md`]

---

## 4. Profit Levers

Direct analysis of where profitability can be moved:

**Pricing power:**
- Current pricing: [observation]
- Pricing-vs-value gap: [analysis]
- Ceiling: [can pricing rise? Only if supported by positioning, scarcity, or transformation value]

**Cost of delivery:**
- Where time/effort goes: [observation]
- Bottlenecks or scope-creep risks: [analysis]
- Opportunities to standardize or automate: [specific moves]

**LTV / CAC leverage:**
- Acquisition cost context: [what it takes to get a buyer]
- Lifetime value opportunities: [renewals, upsells, cross-sells, referrals]
- Ratio health: [if known]

**Packaging / upsell opportunities:**
- Current packaging: [observation]
- Tiering opportunities: [where premium/basic split could increase revenue]
- Natural upsells: [adjacent offers the same audience would buy next]

---

## 5. Recommendations to Increase Profitability

3-5 prioritized, specific, justified recommendations. Each recommendation has:
- A clear heading
- A specific action (not "improve X")
- A rationale grounded in audience psychology, pricing theory, or competitive advantage

### Recommendation 1: [Specific action heading]

**Action:** [Specific, concrete move — not "raise prices" without justification]

**Rationale:** [Why this move works — tied to buyer psychology, pricing theory, or market positioning]

**Expected impact:** [Directional estimate: specific lever moved and by roughly how much]

### Recommendation 2: [Specific action heading]

**Action:** ...

**Rationale:** ...

**Expected impact:** ...

### Recommendation 3: [Specific action heading]

**Action:** ...

**Rationale:** ...

**Expected impact:** ...

### (Recommendations 4-5 as warranted)

---

## 6. Optional Next Steps

[Suggested follow-up actions, prompts, or data that would deepen this review. Not required — include only if there's a genuinely useful next move.]

- [Next step 1]: [why this would matter]
- [Next step 2]: [why]
- [Data to gather]: [what and why]

---

## Optional: Financial-Benefits Lens

*Run this additional lens when the offer lacks clear financial-outcome articulation (buyer can't easily calculate ROI).*

**Does this offer clearly help buyers make or save money?**

[Yes / No / Indirect — with brief analysis]

**If unclear, 5-minute brainstorming exercise to surface the financial benefit:**

1. What does the buyer currently spend/lose by *not* having this offer? [Quantify where possible]
2. What revenue or cost-savings would they realize with the offer? [Specific numbers, even if rough]
3. Over what timeframe would that lift compound? [Months, quarters, lifetime]
4. What would be a conservative estimate of 12-month financial impact? [Anchored dollar figure]
5. How would you articulate this in one sentence? [*"Every month you don't solve [root problem], you're leaving $X in [revenue/savings] on the table."*]

**Repositioning recommendation (if financial-benefits framing is weak):**
[Specific messaging shift that ties the offer to financial outcomes]
```

## Key rules

- **"Don't recommend raise-your-prices unless supported"** — source-stated constraint. The easy recommendation without value justification reads as lazy. Every pricing recommendation must be grounded in positioning, scarcity, or transformation value.
- **"Avoid generalities. Every recommendation must be specific and contextualized."** — no "improve your marketing" or "strengthen your brand." Specific moves, named.
- **"Stay grounded in offer mechanics, not just surface-level messaging."** — messaging tweaks produce temporary lift. Mechanical changes (pricing, packaging, delivery, audience) produce durable improvement. Ground recommendations in the mechanics, not the copy.
- **Tone: sharp, decisive, professional.** The user is unoffendable and wants the best outcome. Critique honestly.
- **Financial-benefits lens is optional.** Run it when the offer lacks clear revenue/cost articulation. Don't force it on every review.
- **Hybrid analytical perspective** — pricing psychology (value perception) + full-funnel marketing (acquisition path) + behavioral economics (decision friction). Each section should reflect at least one of these lenses. Missing any produces shallow analysis.

## Grounding check before shipping

1. Is every recommendation specific, prioritized, and justified?
2. Are pricing recommendations supported by positioning / scarcity / transformation value evidence?
3. Is the Market Fit section honest about gaps (not just confirming the offer owner's assumptions)?
4. Does the review ground in offer mechanics, not surface messaging?
5. If the financial-benefits lens ran, are the numbers anchored (even rough) rather than vague?
