# Offer Frameworks — Strategic Reference

Cross-cutting domain frameworks used across `offer` modes. Compact reference for concepts that recur: vehicle-vs-method, root-problem-vs-symptoms, high-ticket psychology, 3-pillar vehicle structure, and Hormozi's offer-worthy-market criteria. Complements the deeper playbooks (`unique-mechanism-playbook.md` and `vehicle-theory.md`) with strategic discipline.

## Vehicle vs. Method distinction

Direct-response tradition (surfaced most explicitly in NTPV Designer). These are often confused, and the confusion weakens offers.

- **Vehicle** = the **logical framework** — the overarching system or approach that makes logical sense to your prospect. Explains *why* your way works differently.
- **Method** = the **tactical execution** — the specific steps, processes, and tactics *inside* the vehicle.

Examples:

| Vehicle (what sells the appointment) | Method (what closes the sale) |
|---|---|
| The Compound Authority System | 1. Authority audit 2. Content pillar architecture 3. Strategic partnership mapping 4. Thought-leadership calendar 5. Speaking pipeline 6. Media mention system |
| Reverse Funnel Methodology | (tactical funnel steps) |
| 90-Day Sprint Framework | (weekly execution cadence) |
| Micro-Commitment Sequencing | (sequence of low-friction asks) |

**Why the distinction matters:**

- **The Vehicle sells the appointment.** It's what gets prospects curious and wanting to learn more. It needs to be logically sound, easy to understand conceptually, and different from what they've seen before.
- **The Method closes the sale.** It's the proof you can actually deliver. It needs to be specific, proprietary, and systematized.
- **The common mistake:** most people create a Method without a Vehicle. *"I have a 12-step process to get more leads"* is a method. *"I use the Magnetic Authority Framework — a system that turns expertise into client magnetism through strategic positioning rather than traditional marketing"* is a Vehicle, inside which there's a Method.
- **Psychology:** Vehicle = gives them a new way to *think* about their problem. Method = gives them confidence you can *solve* their problem.

In practice: **create a Vehicle FIRST, then build Methods inside it.** A Method without a Vehicle competes on price. A Vehicle with a Method inside it escapes price comparison.

Naming constraint: never use marketing names for the Vehicle ("The Ultimate Success System"). Use logical descriptors that immediately convey the mechanism ("Revenue Acceleration Framework," "Authority Compound System"). Cross-reference naming discipline in `unique-mechanism-playbook.md`.

## Root problem vs. symptoms

Where most marketers fail (NTPV Designer's framing).

- **Symptoms** are what people complain about. *"I work 80 hours a week."*
- **Root problems** are what actually cause the symptoms. *"No systems for delegation."*

High-ticket buyers want root-problem solutions, not symptom-treatment. *"How to work fewer hours"* reads as a symptom pitch. *"How to build delegation systems that let you step out of operations"* reads as a root-problem pitch.

In offer, this surfaces as:

- **Diagnostic discipline** — before constructing the offer, identify which problem the prospect *thinks* they have vs. which root problem actually causes it.
- **Mechanism alignment** — the Unique Mechanism should target the root problem. Mechanisms that address only symptoms produce temporary lift that doesn't compound.
- **Messaging alignment** — sales copy names both the symptom (recognition) and the root (credibility). *"You don't have a time problem. You have a delegation problem — and here's the system that fixes it."*

## High-ticket psychology ($3,000+)

High-ticket (typically $3K+, requiring consultative selling) has a different buyer psychology than low-ticket:

- **Buyers are sophisticated and skeptical.** They've seen marketing speak. They detect hype instantly. Generic claims backfire.
- **They have urgent pain + immediate budget + decision authority.** Top 1% of any market — not "everyone," not "anyone with interest."
- **They've been burned before.** Past failed investments make them cautious. Proof matters more than hype.
- **They need logical justification plus emotional connection.** Emotional resonance alone reads as manipulation at this price point. Logic alone reads as cold. Both are required.
- **They need proof.** Specific client names, exact numbers, verifiable results. *"John's revenue went from $500K to $1.2M in 6 months"* beats *"John loves our program."*
- **They need authority positioning.** Must be THE expert, not AN expert. Content, credentials, case studies, consistent messaging that positions the seller as the obvious choice in the niche.

**Niching down is the counter-intuitive move.** *"I help SaaS founders scale to $10M ARR"* beats *"I help entrepreneurs grow their business"* in high-ticket. But don't over-niche — getting too specific constrains the addressable market. The goal is tight enough that the prospect recognizes themselves immediately, broad enough to sustain a business.

These principles apply most directly to `os-offer/modes/offer-vehicle` and `os-offer/modes/unique-mechanism` when the offer is positioned at high-ticket price points.

## 3-Pillar Vehicle Structure

From NTPV Designer. Every vehicle benefits from exactly 3 pillars (not 2, not 5).

Each pillar is:
- **A differential and logical promise** that touches a specific avatar pain point
- **Includes a unique vehicle component or method** and ideally social proof
- **Explained in simple language** (how the system works without technicalities)
- **Clearly and logically justified** — the prospect should grasp why this pillar is necessary

Example (from NTPV Designer's high-ticket fitness offer):

1. **Pillar #1: Metabolism & Lifestyle Audit** — diagnoses and reverses metabolic suppression through personalized analysis. Unique element: anti-restriction philosophy. Social proof: Dani, age 48: 1,500 calories → 4,000 calories with visible abs.
2. **Pillar #2: Sustainable Nutrition + Minimal Training Protocol** — enjoyable eating plan + recovery-focused 2-3 day training. Unique element: high food volume that surprises clients. Social proof: Juan Carlos, age 50: daily training (no results) → 3 sessions/week, -20 kilos with abs.
3. **Pillar #3: Identity Transformation + 1-on-1 Accountability** — builds lasting habits through mindset shifts and personal support. Unique element: identity-based change vs. willpower reliance.

**Why 3 specifically:**
- Fewer than 3 pillars feels thin — the vehicle doesn't feel complete.
- More than 3 pillars dilutes — prospects can't remember them, and each pillar carries less weight.
- 3 is a cognitive sweet spot (same reason Todd Brown's 75/25 mechanism tends to have 3-5 causal steps, and the Models Method favors triangles for "all three required" relationships).

Cross-reference with Models Method geometric principles in `../../os-biz-strategy/references/models-method.md` — a 3-pillar vehicle often pairs well with a triangular visual model.

## Hormozi's offer-worthy market criteria

From Alex Hormozi $100M Offer Generator. An offer worth making targets a market with all four properties:

1. **Massive pain** — the problem is acute, not "nice-to-have." The prospect feels the cost of not solving it daily.
2. **Available purchasing power** — the prospect has budget. Broke markets don't buy, no matter how good the offer.
3. **Easy to target** — the prospect can be reached through identifiable channels, groups, or content. Un-targetable markets can't be reached at scale.
4. **Growing market** — demand is expanding, not shrinking. Declining markets shrink your addressable audience over time.

Offers that miss any of the four have structural weakness. Missing *massive pain* produces a vitamin (nice but skippable). Missing *purchasing power* produces a good idea that no one buys. Missing *easy to target* produces CAC that eats margin. Missing *growing market* produces an offer that peaks then declines.

When brainstorming offers in `modes/offer-brainstorm` depth pattern, filter against these four criteria explicitly.

## Malleability note

Reference-grade but malleable. The distinctions and frameworks here are direct-response tradition canon. What IS malleable:

- **3-Pillar vehicle structure** — exact-3 is a strong default but not dogmatic. A very compact offer (single-session intensive) may need only 2 pillars. A sprawling multi-phase program may warrant 4 if the fourth is clearly distinct. Avoid 5+ under almost any circumstance.
- **Hormozi's four criteria** — required for a grand-slam offer but not a gating test. Some offers (premium niche) intentionally serve a smaller market (missing #3) for positioning reasons.
- **High-ticket psychology** — applies most clearly to $3K+ offers. Below that threshold, buyer psychology compresses and some principles soften. Scale the rigor to the price point.

Skills that diverge should note the reason in their Design Rationale.
