# Content-Strategy Stance — what the EIB modes bias toward

Content-discovery's EIB modes (`topics`, `subtopics`, `drivers`) operate with a deliberate content-strategy bias. This document names the bias openly so users can adjust when their audience needs different defaults.

## The default bias

The modes default to surfacing content that works for online business audiences — solopreneurs, entrepreneurs, creators, consultants, infoproduct sellers, ambitious professionals.

For these audiences, content that engages tends to live in:

- Competitive edge (how to win, how to stand out, how to outperform)
- Money-making (revenue, monetization, pricing, sales tactics)
- Productivity (time management, focus, energy management, systems)
- Mental mastery (mindset, identity, self-talk, confidence)
- Mental models (frameworks, principles, decision rubrics)
- Business frameworks (positioning, branding, offer structure)
- Immediately actionable tips (concrete moves the reader can try today)
- Surprising counter-intuitive takes (challenging conventional wisdom)
- Personal story → universal lesson (anecdote that illuminates a principle)

Content that tends to *not* engage these audiences (despite being important in other contexts):

- Ethics / morality discussions
- Sustainability / environmental topics
- Inclusiveness / DEI as a topic
- Data-driven decision-making as a meta-topic
- Economic uncertainty / macroeconomic commentary
- Generic motivational content with no specific takeaway

The modes default to surfacing topics, subtopics, and FFGAs in the engagement-friendly territory and away from the engagement-resistant territory.

## Why this is a content-strategy bias, not a values judgment

The bias is about what produces scroll-stopping, share-worthy, lead-generating content for the most common downstream audience (online business creators), not about what's important to discuss in life.

Sustainability, ethics, and inclusiveness are important. They're also typically not what stops the scroll on LinkedIn or earns the share on Twitter for an audience focused on growing their business. Content strategists know this, and the modes reflect that knowledge.

When the audience explicitly cares about these topics — a sustainability-focused B-corp, an ethics-in-AI consultant, a DEI training company — the modes adjust. The bias is a *default*, not a constraint.

## Adjusting for different audiences

The user can override the default by:

- Stating the override directly: *"include ethics-related topics, my audience cares about that"*
- Defining an audience whose desired outcome is in the override territory: *"sustainability consultants who want to land enterprise clients"* — the mode will produce sustainability-leaning topics
- Supplying calibration notes: *"focus on mindset and identity work, less tactical"*

The modes honor overrides without commentary. If a user wants ethics content, the modes produce ethics content.

## How the bias shows up

Each EIB mode applies the bias slightly differently:

**`topics`** filters at the pillar level. *"Workplace ethics"* won't show up as a default pillar for a generic entrepreneurship audience — *"Building a values-aligned business"* might (more story-shaped, more identity-level).

**`subtopics`** replaces would-be ethics-y subtopics with problem-solving subtopics. *"Why DEI matters for solopreneurs"* gets replaced by *"How to build genuine connection with customers without fake-engineered diversity messaging"* — the same territory but framed for engagement rather than abstraction.

**`drivers`** replaces ethics-driven feelings with feelings around solving specific problems. *"I'm worried about my carbon footprint"* won't appear as a default fear — *"I'm worried I'm going to look hypocritical to my audience if I don't get this aligned"* might (the same identity-level concern, framed as audience-perception rather than abstract ethics).

## The replace-with-specific-problem rule

When a candidate output drifts into the engagement-resistant territory by default, the mode replaces it with a more specific-problem-focused version of the same underlying concern. This is the *"replace ethics with specific problem"* heuristic preserved from the source EIB prompts.

The replacement isn't about hiding ethics — it's about translating abstract concerns into concrete pains the audience would actually name. Concrete pains are what engaging content addresses.
