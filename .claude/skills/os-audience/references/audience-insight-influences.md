# Named Domain Influences — Conversion Psychology & Direct Response

Domain context loaded by `audience` modes that cite named experts. Influences are preserved as **pointers to real bodies of work**, not as personas for the agent to cosplay. When a mode mentions an influence, it's signaling a tradition the user can consult — the agent should synthesize the concept, not impersonate the author.

This pattern was adopted during the voiceprint conversion: source prompts that framed experts as personas to emulate (*"You synthesize the techniques of X, Y, and Z"*) were rewritten to keep the experts as context while dropping the persona-cosplay framing. Frontier models don't measurably benefit from cosplay. They do benefit from knowing which tradition a concept comes from so they can reason about it accurately.

## The roster

- **Dan Kennedy** — direct-response tradition. *No B.S. Marketing Letter*, *The Ultimate Sales Letter*. Source of the aggressive-clarity, specificity-over-fluff copy standard.
- **Eugene Schwartz** — *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966). Source of the 5 Stages of Awareness ladder. Canon for stage-matched messaging. See `psychographic-frameworks.md`.
- **Russell Brunson** — *DotCom Secrets*, *Expert Secrets*. Source of the "Big Domino" belief concept, hook-story-offer structure, and the belief-bridging framework that underpins the four belief categories in `buyer-psychology-beliefs.md`.
- **Alex Hormozi** — *$100M Offers*. Source of the Value Equation (see `psychographic-frameworks.md`). Source of value-stacking and grand-slam-offer design principles.
- **Blair Warren** — *The One Sentence Persuasion Course*. Source of belief-based persuasion — "people will do anything for those who encourage their dreams, justify their failures, allay their fears, confirm their suspicions, and help them throw rocks at their enemies."
- **Amy Porterfield** — online course / lead magnet strategy, particularly for coaches and solopreneurs. Source of micro-transformation-in-lead-magnet patterns.
- **Marie Forleo** — conversational persuasion and permission-based sales. Source of the "approachable expert" positioning tone.
- **Taki Moore** — coach marketing. Source of the "coaching-sales-mechanic" approach and Black Belt / Boardroom frameworks.
- **Brian Clark** — Copyblogger. Source of the content-as-marketing tradition and the "education-first" lead magnet philosophy.
- **Donald Miller** — *Building a StoryBrand*. Source of the clarity-driven storytelling framework and the before/after transformation framing used in `modes/core-transformation`.
- **Nancy Duarte** — *Resonate*. Source of the contrast/tension narrative structure — the oscillation between what-is and what-could-be.
- **Brené Brown** — shame research, vulnerability. Source of the emotional-truth layer that informs the "Hidden Desires" and identity-level work in `modes/avatar`.

## How to cite influences in mode output

When a mode needs to reference an influence by name (e.g., "Applying Schwartz's awareness ladder..."), cite briefly and move on. Do not lengthen the output with biography. The user knows (or can look up) who the person is. The value is in the concept applied to their specific situation.

Do **not** output sentences like *"You synthesize the belief-dismantling techniques of Russell Brunson with the emotional resonance frameworks of Brené Brown."* — that's residue from the persona-cosplay era. If a concept comes from a tradition, say so, use the concept, move on.

## Used by

All modes, when relevant. Most commonly cited: Schwartz (for awareness stages), Hormozi (for value articulation), Brunson (for belief categories), Miller (for FROM/TO transformation framing).
