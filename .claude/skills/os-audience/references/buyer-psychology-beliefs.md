# Buyer Psychology — Belief Categories, Excavation, and Dislodge Principles

Domain reference loaded by multiple `audience` modes. Shared content, each mode uses a different slice.

## The four belief categories

A working model for classifying limiting beliefs that prevent a prospect from buying. Drawn from sales-copy and conversion-psychology tradition (Russell Brunson's "Big Domino", Vehicle / Internal / External decomposition from belief-bridging psychology).

### Big Domino Beliefs

The two foundational beliefs that, if shifted, cause downstream objections to collapse. These are the "aha" shifts — large enough to anchor a 90-minute webinar or a book. When a Big Domino falls, the prospect's remaining resistance is rendered moot.

Typical form: a macro-level worldview claim. *"There's no way to X without Y."* *"People like me don't get to have Z."* *"The only path to my outcome is the expensive path."*

### Vehicle Beliefs

Doubts about the *method, system, or process* behind the specific offer. "Will this actually work?" skepticism tied to the mechanism.

Typical form: *"This kind of approach has been tried — it doesn't work for my situation."* *"I've seen other people try X. They failed."* *"The mechanism sounds gimmicky."*

### Internal Beliefs

Self-doubt. Fears about the buyer's own capability to succeed with the method. Perceived inadequacy, identity friction, past failures projected forward.

Typical form: *"I'm not disciplined enough."* *"I've tried similar things and failed — I'm the problem."* *"People who make this work are different from me."*

### External Beliefs

Doubts about outside circumstances — time, money, support system, economy, industry conditions, partner/family, geography.

Typical form: *"I don't have the time."* *"I can't afford it right now."* *"My spouse would never go along with this."* *"The economy is bad."*

## The belief-behind-the-belief

Surface beliefs are proxies for the deeper beliefs that actually drive the decision — the ones the prospect won't state directly. Excavation means asking, for each surface belief: *what is the prospect really afraid of, protecting, or avoiding?*

Examples:
- **Surface:** *"I don't have the time."* → **Belief-behind:** *"If I commit and fail, I'll have proved I can't do this — it's safer not to try."*
- **Surface:** *"The economy is bad right now."* → **Belief-behind:** *"I need external permission to spend on myself, and 'the economy' gives me that permission in reverse."*
- **Surface:** *"I'll lose my magic."* → **Belief-behind:** *"My identity is wrapped up in the 1:1 craft, and scaling feels like betraying who I am."*

The belief-behind is where the persuasive work actually happens. Counter the surface belief without touching the belief-behind and the prospect's resistance just migrates to a new surface.

## Reframe vs. counter — the dislodge principle

Belief-shifting moves must *dislodge* the belief, not merely counter it. A counter argues against. A dislodge reframes, replaces, or displaces the belief with something more empowering.

- **Counter (weak):** *"You do have time — here's how to find it."* → the prospect's internal belief-structure is unchanged. They just feel criticized.
- **Dislodge (strong):** *"Time isn't what's scarce. What's scarce is a system that lets you act without more time. Here's what it looks like to have that system."* → the frame shifts, and the original belief no longer has anywhere to stand.

Every counter-framework a skill produces should aim for dislodge. If the output reads like an argument, it's countering. If it reads like a new way of seeing the situation, it's dislodging.

## Reveal-and-restraint — content vs. offer balance

Belief-shifting content for marketing (webinars, VSLs, lead magnets, email sequences) should give away the *strategy* (what and why) and withhold the *tactics* (how). This is not gatekeeping for its own sake — it preserves the value of paid offers while building demand. The prospect walks away believing the shift is real and possible, and needing the paid path to execute.

- **Reveal:** the framework name, the reasoning, why it works, what changes when it's in place, a conceptual overview of the components.
- **Withhold:** step-by-step execution details, specific templates, ordered tactical sequences, the actual deliverables.

Skills that produce belief-shifting assets default to this balance unless the user explicitly overrides (e.g., for paid-customer material where full tactics are appropriate).
