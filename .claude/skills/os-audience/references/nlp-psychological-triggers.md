# NLP Psychological Triggers — 20-Field Excavation

A structured excavation of the prospect's psychological landscape around a specific offer, written in NLP-pattern language. Classic direct-response tradition (Eben Pagan / Frank Kern lineage, exact origin unconfirmed).

Loaded primarily by `modes/limiting-beliefs` in its Deconstruction step. Each field gets a direct, specific answer about the prospect targeted by the input offer — no placeholders, no generalities.

## The 20 fields

### Goals and complaints

- **Primary Goal** — the top-level outcome the prospect wants
- **Secondary Goal 1** — second-order outcome tied to the primary
- **Secondary Goal 2** — third-order outcome tied to the primary
- **Primary Complaint** — the main pain point the prospect names
- **Secondary Complaint 1** — adjacent pain
- **Secondary Complaint 2** — adjacent pain
- **Secondary Complaint 3** — adjacent pain

### Consequences, promises, habits

- **Consequences** — what happens if the prospect doesn't solve this
- **Promises** — what the offer (and adjacent solutions) typically promise
- **Bad Habits** — what the prospect does that perpetuates the problem
- **Works Even If** — conditions/excuses under which the mechanism still works (disarms the "but my situation is different" objection in advance)

### Beliefs and branding

- **Mistaken Beliefs** — surface-level wrong ideas the prospect holds
- **Brand The Mistaken Belief** — a label for the belief (makes it nameable and refutable — 2–4 words)
- **Brand The Primary Cause** — a label for the real underlying cause
- **Brand The Primary Solution** — a label for the reframed solution

### Market context

- **Negative Statistics** — data points that illustrate the severity of the problem
- **False Solutions** — common wrong paths prospects try first
- **Success Myths** — beliefs about what success requires that are false
- **When They Do This = [I Do THIS Instead]** — contrast pairs: what the market does vs. what this offer does

### Outcomes and resistance

- **Benefits** — concrete outcomes the offer produces
- **Objections** — stated reasons the prospect hesitates
- **Dream** — the full-realized best-case outcome, vividly described

## How the fields feed downstream work

**For the 10 Sales Bullets** (next step in `limiting-beliefs`):
- Benefits, Consequences, Works Even If → direct material for bullet content
- Brand The Primary Solution → bullets often name this solution explicitly
- When They Do This = [I Do THIS Instead] → contrast-pair bullets

**For the four belief tables** (final step in `limiting-beliefs`):
- Mistaken Beliefs → Big Domino and Vehicle belief candidates
- Bad Habits → Internal belief candidates (what the prospect is doing that reveals an internal belief)
- False Solutions + Success Myths → Vehicle belief candidates
- Objections → all four categories, sorted by type
- Brand The Mistaken Belief / Primary Cause / Primary Solution → counter-framework names

## On the "branding" fields

*Brand The Mistaken Belief / Primary Cause / Primary Solution* each ask for a nameable label. The discipline is naming — an unnamed belief or cause is hard to argue with. A named one is. *"The Productivity Trap"* is easier to dismantle than *"that thing where you feel busy but aren't making progress."*

Naming is a direct-response craft move. Names should be:
- Short (2–4 words)
- Specific (point at the concrete dynamic)
- Claim-worthy (the prospect should feel seen when they hear it)

## Grounding

Fill every field. Each field gets a short specific answer grounded in the input offer — no placeholders, no generalities, no "it depends." Thin fields signal thin research and will weaken every downstream step.

If the input doesn't give enough material to populate a field concretely, flag it and ask for the missing context rather than inventing.

## Field-list provenance

The 20-field list is drawn from the direct-response and NLP marketing tradition — a synthesis of standard belief-deconstruction fields rather than a single canonical source. The value is in the rigor of working through every field, not in the field names themselves being canonical.
