## Behavior → Belief → Identity Driver

A working mental model for audience analysis: any observed behavior is driven by a belief, which is anchored in an identity driver. Skills that analyze buyer psychology should trace the arc from the surface behavior back to the identity layer — because interventions at the identity layer produce durable shifts, while interventions at the behavior layer produce temporary compliance.

Example trace:
- **Behavior:** the prospect keeps buying courses they never finish.
- **Belief:** *"The right course will finally unlock this for me."*
- **Identity driver:** *"I am a person who learns my way out of problems."* (learning-as-identity rather than action-as-identity)

Reaching the identity driver reveals why the surface behavior keeps recurring — and what a successful intervention has to reframe.

Used by:
- `modes/avatar` — Psychological Landscape section, three-layer fears table uses this arc (Surface → Underlying / Deeper → Root / Identity)
- `modes/dmo` — when analyzing obstacles, trace the observed blocker to the identity level
- `modes/limiting-beliefs` — the Deeper Belief column in the four tables is the identity-driver layer
- `modes/core-transformation` — the transformation is always an identity shift (FROM "person who X" TO "person who Y")

## Framework attributions

- Schwartz's 5 Stages of Awareness — *Breakthrough Advertising* (Eugene Schwartz, 1966)
- Hormozi's Value Equation — *$100M Offers* (Alex Hormozi)
- Behavior → Belief → Identity Driver — direct-response and persuasion tradition (no single canonical source; commonly applied across psychographic profiling work)
