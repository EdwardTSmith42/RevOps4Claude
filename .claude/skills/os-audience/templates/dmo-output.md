# DMO (Desires, Motivators, Obstacles) — canonical output shape

Use this shape for `modes/dmo`. Four sections — the distinguishing feature is the temporal framing of obstacles (Before / During / After the offer).

## Shape

```markdown
# DMO Analysis: [Target Audience]

## 1. Core Desires & Motivators

**Primary Desires Driving This Audience**
- [tangible payoff 1 — financial, status, transformation, security, ease]
- [tangible payoff 2]
- [emotional payoff 1]
- [emotional payoff 2]

**Hidden Emotional Triggers & Identity Factors**
- [subconscious motivation 1 — what they don't name publicly but drives behavior]
- [identity factor 1 — who they want to become, not just what they want to have]
- [subconscious motivation 2]

**How They Define Success**
- [their own definition of "I made it" — specific and first-person]
- [their own definition of an ideal outcome]

---

## 2. Dream Outcomes (via Hormozi's Value Equation)

*See `references/psychographic-frameworks.md` on the Value Equation.*

**Ultimate Transformation**
[What their life or business looks like *after* achieving their goal. Specific, vivid, narrative-grade.]

**Aspirational Goals (Dreamed About, Rarely Verbalized)**
- [the thing they daydream about but don't say out loud]
- [another aspiration they'd be mildly embarrassed to admit]
- [an aspiration they don't even name to themselves]

**External Drivers of the Dream**
- [circumstantial / status / visible-to-others reason they want this]
- ...

**Internal Drivers of the Dream**
- [identity / self-concept / internal reason they want this]
- ...

---

## 3. Obstacles & Pain Points (Before, During, and After the Offer)

**Before — What's stopping them now**
- [knowledge gap, time, self-doubt, skepticism, resources, priors]
- [fear of failure — specific version]
- [lack of right guidance or model]
- ...

**Before — What leads them to search for a solution**
[The precipitating moment or realization. What happened last week, last quarter, at 3 AM that made them start looking.]

**During — What they'll face inside the offer**
- [execution friction — overwhelm, perfectionism, technical hurdle]
- [relational friction — accountability, vulnerability, asking for help]
- [belief friction — "what if I'm the exception who can't do this"]

**After — Secondary obstacles post-fulfillment**
- [maintenance problem — the skill requires ongoing effort]
- [success-contingent problem — new problems the solution creates]
- [identity problem — they don't know who they are without the original pain]

---

## 4. Market Frictions & Psychological Roadblocks

**Why They Hesitate or Delay**
- [friction 1]
- [friction 2]
- [friction 3]

**Common Objections and Excuses**
- *"[objection as stated]"* — [what's underneath]
- *"[objection as stated]"* — [what's underneath]
- ...

**Beliefs & Industry Misconceptions Blocking Action**
- [misconception 1 and why it persists]
- [misconception 2 and why it persists]
- ...

**External Factors Affecting Decision**
- [competitive / economic / peer-influence / cultural factor 1]
- [factor 2]
- ...
```

## Key rules

- **Before / During / After is not optional.** The temporal framing of obstacles is the mode's distinguishing craft move. Most analyses flatten obstacles into a single list. The temporal split reveals post-purchase risks that flat analysis misses.
- **External and Internal drivers, both.** Dream Outcomes must be unpacked into both layers. Any desire has a circumstantial driver (what others will see, what it buys) and an identity driver (who it makes them). Missing either produces thin articulation.
- **Grounded specificity over generality.** *"Self-doubt"* is a category, not content. *"Self-doubt that they're the exception who can't sustain a system"* is content.
- **Objections are quoted in voice.** First-person quotes match how the prospect would actually phrase the objection — not paraphrased into third-person analyst-speak.
- **Hidden emotional triggers are the unstated material.** If the user could have told you this themselves, you haven't dug far enough. See `references/buyer-psychology-beliefs.md` on belief-behind-the-belief.

## Optional extension: compact variant

For lighter-touch use (quick sanity-check on a new audience), collapse into a three-section output:

```markdown
# DMO Compact: [Audience]

**Desires** (top 5, ranked): ...
**Motivators** (top 5): ...
**Obstacles** (5+, Before/During/After noted in parens): ...
```

Default is the full four-section output. Use compact when explicitly requested or chained into another mode.
