# Avatar — canonical output shape

Use this shape as the default output for `modes/avatar`. The structure merges the two legacy avatar formats (Avatar-Maker AI's base shape + Audience Profiler for Ads's richer variant) into a single richer template.

## Shape

```markdown
# Avatar: [target audience — a short descriptive phrase that names the segment by its predicament, not its demographic. e.g. a state of stuckness, not a job title.]

## Persona Spotlight: Meet [alliterative first name], the [emotional identity role — capitalized descriptor that names the predicament; the kind of person they are inside the situation, not their profession]

**Profile:**
- [age, life-stage, role, identifying markers — one line, demographic-thin; this section is the lightest in the whole avatar]
- [the goals they admit publicly — the socially-acceptable version, what they'd put on LinkedIn]
- [the goals they don't admit — the version they'd only own up to with a close friend, often involving status, recognition, or escape]
- [the dominant emotions running underneath their daily self-talk — name them as feelings, not categories. "Resentment at being needed but invisible" beats "frustration."]

[A 3–5-sentence narrative scene of their day-to-day reality. Specific time of day, specific action, specific tension or unmet desire. Written in scene, not summary — name what's on the screen, what's in the room, what isn't getting done. The narrative is the depth gauge: if you can't picture the moment, the avatar isn't grounded enough yet. Generic produces generic copy downstream.]
```

## Key rules

- **Alliterative persona name.** A first name and a role-descriptor that share an initial sound — the alliteration makes the avatar memorable and nameable. Not required if a non-alliterative name fits the segment better, but default to alliterative.
- **Emotional Identity Role, not a job title.** A capitalized descriptor that names the predicament (the kind of person they are inside the situation), not the demographic role. A demographic label tells you what they do for work; an emotional identity role tells you what they're stuck inside. Downstream copy targets the predicament.
- **Hidden Truth column is the 4th column**, not skippable. This is where the output earns its keep. The Current vs. Desired gap is obvious. The *why they haven't closed it* is the hidden truth.
- **"What They Won't Tell Anyone" framing is literal.** What prospects state publicly is a filtered signal. Push past that to what they'd only admit at 3 AM or to a therapist.
- **Three-layer fear excavation** (Surface → Underlying → Deep-Seated / Root). Each layer should be distinctly deeper. Identity-level fears at the root, not circumstantial ones.
- **Three sub-categories for Dream Outcomes** (Emotional / Lifestyle / Identity). Forces the output past "make more money" into specific feelings, specific markers, specific identity shifts.
- **Grounded specificity over generality** throughout. *"Gets 147 unread emails and hasn't touched her course outline for three weeks"* beats *"feels overwhelmed."* Specific beats abstract.
- **Closing line summarizes the internal arc**, not the business case. The avatar is a portrait of a person, not a marketing persona.

## What this template is not

- **Not a demographic profile.** Age and role markers are one line, not the whole document. The work is psychographic — the unstated, unfiltered, identity-level material.
- **Not a list of topics to write about.** The avatar feeds downstream content/offer skills, and those skills decide what to say. The avatar profiles who is listening.
- **Not quotes from the source input.** Any first-person quotes in the template (like in "What Keeps Them Awake") are the analyst's synthesis of what the prospect WOULD say — not what any real person in the input has actually said.

## Grounding check before shipping

1. Is every cell populated with specific, grounded content?
2. Does the avatar feel like a person someone could run into at a coffee shop, not a persona-segment label?
3. Does the Hidden Truth column reveal something the prospect wouldn't readily name themselves?
4. Do the fears and values tensions feel identity-level, not circumstantial?

If any answer is no, the avatar isn't done yet.
