# Angle Cluster Taxonomy

Five clusters that describe the job-to-be-done of a piece of content, independent of its delivery format. Used in `content-mining` Step 7 to organize generated content angles.

Unlike `../../_shared/references/content-format-taxonomy.md` (which classifies *how* content is delivered), this classifies *what the content is trying to do* for the reader and the creator.

## The five clusters

### Education

Content that teaches. The reader ends up knowing or being able to do something they couldn't before.

**Jobs this serves:**
- Build the reader's skill or knowledge
- Answer questions the audience actually has
- Make a domain accessible to newcomers

**What angles in this cluster do:**
- Lay out the full landscape of a domain so the reader gets oriented in one piece
- Explain the underlying mechanism of a concept the reader already half-knows
- Walk through a specific task with enough detail that the reader could do it next

### Persuasion

Content that changes minds. The reader ends up believing something they didn't — or no longer believing something they did.

**Jobs this serves:**
- Challenge defaults in the field
- Flip a belief the audience is holding
- Argue for a non-obvious position with reasoning

**What angles in this cluster do:**
- Take a default belief in the field and argue against it with reasoning the reader can follow
- Stake out a non-obvious position and build the case so the reader can adopt or reject it on merits
- Name a piece of conventional wisdom the audience holds and dismantle it

### Authority

Content that signals expertise. The reader ends up trusting the creator more — not by being told to, but by being shown something that only someone with real experience could say.

**Jobs this serves:**
- Demonstrate depth of domain knowledge
- Show the creator's POV on contested questions
- Separate the creator from the field

**What angles in this cluster do:**
- Surface aspects of the domain that only an insider would name
- Strip the marketing veneer off what a particular role actually does day to day
- Take the reader behind a decision that looked simple from outside and show what it actually involved

### Narrative

Content that tells a story. The reader ends up having lived through something — the story's lesson is absorbed through the experience, not lectured.

**Jobs this serves:**
- Build emotional connection
- Make abstract principles concrete through lived experience
- Invite the reader into a transformation they can imagine for themselves

**What angles in this cluster do:**
- Trace a transformation arc the reader can map onto their own situation
- Locate a single turning-point moment and unpack what changed in it
- Tell the lived backstory behind an outcome the reader has only seen the surface of

### Tactical

Content that gets a specific result. The reader ends up able to take a precise action today and see an outcome tomorrow.

**Jobs this serves:**
- Provide immediately-applicable moves
- Answer "what should I do this week?"
- Transfer specific, transferable tactics

**What angles in this cluster do:**
- Hand the reader a literal script or template they can adapt to a recurring situation
- Name a small fixed set of moves that resolve a specific stuck-point
- Compress a tactic into a tight time-bound walkthrough the reader could try the same day

## How to use the taxonomy

In Step 7 of `content-mining`:
- Generate content angles from the primitives produced in Steps 2–6
- Tag each angle with a cluster
- Check distribution — if all angles fall into one cluster, take one honest second look at the others; sources often carry implied persuasion or narrative material the first pass misses
- If the second look comes up dry, let the skew stand. A dominant cluster is a finding about the source, not an extraction failure — never manufacture an angle to round out the distribution

### Overlaps with the format taxonomy

Angle clusters describe **intent**. Format taxonomy describes **delivery mode**. An "education" cluster angle could be delivered as a practical how-to, a thought leadership piece, or a lessons listicle. Use both axes when planning a content system.

Example cross-classification:
- Angle: "The step-by-step for naming your offer so it sells" → Cluster: Education → Format: Practical how-to
- Angle: "Why most offer-naming advice makes things worse" → Cluster: Persuasion → Format: Thought leadership
- Angle: "How I named my flagship offer and doubled conversions" → Cluster: Narrative → Format: Lessons listicle
- Angle: "My 15-minute offer-naming checklist" → Cluster: Tactical → Format: Advice

Same source, four different posts, four different jobs.

## Malleability note

Reference-grade but malleable. The five clusters capture one cut at the jobs-to-be-done of content. The *kind* of intent-classification matters more than the exact five. Skills that want a different cut (adding an "entertainment" cluster, merging persuasion and authority, splitting tactical into scripts vs. checklists) may diverge. Note the reason when they do.
