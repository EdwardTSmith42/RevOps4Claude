Definition

Connects now run on duration, at two different bars

3 min / 10 min

A call connect is a dial of 3 minutes or more; a demo connect is a recording of 10 minutes or more, and a demo under that is counted a no-show. Approved by Mel Matthews and Ed Smith. The old single 5-minute gate failed because it was applied to both populations at once — on this window it clears just 8% of real outbound dials but 96% of meeting recordings. Two bars on two populations fixes that. HubSpot's own automation still runs on disposition, so both numbers are reported side by side and neither is a correction of the other.

        Known data-quality limits

        Before anyone quotes these numbers

        Known limits of this build.
        • A demo is a meeting whose title contains “demo”. That is the definition behind Laura's existing Demos dashboard (21888050) and it reproduces her numbers. It is not the hs_activity_type picklist, which is set on only 62 of the 591 demo meetings in this window. The demo count did not move on 2026-09-20 — the 10-minute rule splits it into demo connects and no-shows, it does not filter it.
        • No-shows are inferred from recording length, not from attendance. Ed Smith ruled on 2026-09-20 that a demo whose recording ran under ten minutes is marked a no-show. That is an interim proxy: nothing in this data records who actually turned up. Laura is building real attendance data from the meeting records and it is not backfilled yet; when it lands it should replace this rule. Until then, read a single rep's no-show count as a signal to go and look at the calendar, never as a fact about a specific prospect. The retired 5-minute “performed” gate is worse, not better: a recording bot keeps running while the AE waits alone: of the 155 demos whose contact is hand-flagged No Show, 104 ran ten minutes or longer, so the lower five-minute bar would have passed all 104 of them as “performed”.
        • — calls in this window have no contact association and cannot land on any contact row — and therefore land in no daily bucket and in no period. Team totals include them; the contact list and every period figure above cannot. Effectively all of them are the SDR's dialer calls.
        • Per-contact dial counts are slightly inflated. 23% of calls associate to more than one contact, so a single dial can appear on several contact rows. The rep scorecard and the two-seats cards are also contact grain — they are summed from the per-rep daily buckets, which hang off contact rows — so they carry the same inflation, as the note above that table says. Only the call-grain rows in “Where every number comes from” are unaffected.
        • The contact→company link is unreliable. — of the contacts in this window have no company or are linked to many, and a few are linked to hundreds (one to 621). The primary company is resolved with associatedcompanyid, which matches HubSpot's own Primary association exactly. Every company-grain figure on this page — sourced enrollments on the Combined card, the rep scorecard's enrollment column, the revenue cohort — is capped by this.
        • Enrollment is company-grain and week-accurate, not day-accurate. enrollment_date is stamped from the 06:31 CT nightly ELI sync, so it lands a day late — corrected by −1 day here. 45% of volume still arrives in catch-up batches, so read it weekly or monthly, never daily. It only exists from 2026-05-13.
        • — Aloware calls in this window have no owner in HubSpot, and it is two separate causes. (a) Unanswered inbound rings never get an owner, because no agent handled them - structural, and always true. (b) Outbound calls from the newest reps arrive unowned because their Aloware seats are not linked to their HubSpot users. Brandon and Gabrielle, whose seats are linked, have zero unowned outbound. It does not self-heal - old calls are still unowned today. We recover the rep from the call body and, where no name is written, from the rep’s own line number; —. Whatever is not recovered is Local-Presence outbound where neither the body nor the caller ID names a rep, and it is missing from the per-rep daily buckets that drive the rep scorecard, so that table is a floor per rep.
        • 46 calls (4.6%) recorded only hold-tone audio. All are long Mel/Gabrielle meeting recordings (11-15 minutes) where the recorder captured touch-tone menu navigation and no speech. They are logged as long connects and will inflate any talk-time metric built on call duration. Root cause looks like a recording defect rather than rep behaviour, and is unconfirmed.
        • The previous transcript run over-counted usable calls. Its filter measured raw characters including the speaker-label prefixes, so hold-tone transcripts passed as substantive. 11 of that run's 400 files were below the real bar. This refresh measures speech with labels stripped and verified none remain.
        • Email counts are a floor. The search API caps at 10k per query; this pull unions weekly slices to get past it, but a shared thread still attaches to every contact on it.
