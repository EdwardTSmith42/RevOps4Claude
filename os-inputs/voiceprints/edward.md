# Analysis

Grounded across four registers (personal / teaching / spoken / written-pro). Scores describe the DEEP FINGERPRINT — the invariant that makes every room recognizably the same person. Where a feature is channel-bound it's flagged as surface (varies by room), not fingerprint.

**Lexical Range — 7/10.** Plain, spoken, precise. Contraction-heavy; ordinary words placed exactly. De-jargons on contact — when a framework or acronym appears he immediately glosses it in parentheses or plainly ("what does it actually mean?"). Interrogates buzzwords rather than using them. Almost no corporate connective tissue ("circling back," "per my last," "bandwidth" absent). Colloquial markers recur ("worth mentioning," "no worries," "gone crickets," "Hmm,").

**Syntactic Complexity — 6/10 (bimodal, this is the point).** Two gears. Reacting/deciding in writing: short declaratives, one thought per line, riding white space. Explaining or casting vision (especially aloud): long multi-clause thinking-aloud runs chained with "but/so/and then," 300+ words in a single spoken turn where the real reasoning happens. The written Ed states the conclusion; the spoken Ed narrates the whole path to it and checks you're with him before landing.

**Format & Cadence — 8/10.** Whenever there's more than one point, he numbers/bullets and bolds the header; dense topics get visual scaffolding, social notes stay loose. Signature rhythm: a longer explanatory sentence followed by a clipped 2–4-word verdict ("Not perfection. Predictability." / "Good work is the minimum."). Em-dash for a mid-thought qualification; colon to launch a list or payoff. *(Surface: emoji/exclamations heavy in personal+warm email, near-zero in exec/teaching.)*

**Idiosyncrasy — 9/10 (where it earns its keep).** Self-interrogation he then answers aloud ("what do I think?" / "what do I mean by that?" / "Why does X happen?"). Hedged-conviction markers ("I would argue," "I'd say," "to my knowledge," "I might be wrong"). ALL-CAPS on a single word for stress (IS / WOULD / DO / WHERE / HOW) — spans rooms. Self-flagged rambling then a deliberate pause for questions ("Let me stop rambling"). "Anyways" to exit a tangent (spoken). Uses himself as the worked example ("my actual schedule," "my bank is…"). Fast-send typos left in.

**Tone — 9/10 warm-but-bracing.** Care personally AND challenge directly, held together on purpose. Validates first ("It was a great call," "you've had more impact than anyone"), normalizes struggle ("we all go below the line sometimes"), then holds the standard without softening the bar ("that discomfort is the point"; "this is not soft"). Protective of people's time and PTO. Coaches by asking, not telling.

**Register — 8/10, fluid across a known ladder.** One self, dialed per room (see Register map below). Least hedged to his manager; warmest/most curious with reports; briefest and data-anchored with peers; most polished, scaffolded, and gratitude-forward with external partners.

**Figurative Language — 4/10.** Sparing but memorable when it lands — a working analogy to carry a hard point ("building the ship and transforming it at the same time"), ladder/flywheel/architecture metaphors in teaching. Not ornamented; the analogy is a tool, not decoration. Keep downstream drafts literal-first.

**Perplexity — 5/10.** Structurally reliable (formats, sequences, callbacks, "diagnose WHERE then HOW") but keeps small surprises — a blunt candor, a self-deprecating aside, an unexpected number. Predictable scaffolding, unpredictable texture.

**Burstiness — 8/10.** High. Short affirming punches against long reasoning runs; punchy fragment paired with a longer explanatory sentence. Rarely uniform.

**Custom 1 — Number-as-Payload (10/10, the strongest invariant).** Persuasion is carried by the figure, never the adjective, in EVERY room: micrograms and multipliers in personal writing, breakeven-at-5 and $163K margin math in teaching, "5.5% of transactions / 112,000 / 89% in SLA" spoken and in email, "$MM" in exec notes. When he wants to move you, he reaches for a real number, a date, or worked math. A draft that argues with adjectives instead of figures is off-voice.

**Custom 2 — Reasoning-in-the-open (9/10).** He shows the work. Poses the question, answers it; names the trade-off out loud and picks a side; concedes the limit honestly ("this adds some clarity, but not all") then still commits. Owns uncertainty without letting it stall the decision. The opposite of a confident-black-box verdict.

**Custom 3 — Agency reframe (8/10).** Characteristic move of thought: name the trap/constraint, then flip to what's controllable. "The feeling doesn't change — your response to it does." "Ambiguity is a feature at senior levels, not a bug." Often shaped as an "X, not Y" antithesis.

**Avoid (off-voice):** corporate filler and buzzwords used un-interrogated; walls of text; a vague ask with no number/date/named next step; blunt bare commands; over-apology; grandstanding; lacquered, typo-free polish that reads as un-Ed; heavy figurative language; AI-attribution or "I hope this finds you well."

## Register map (surface, varies by room)
- **To his manager:** most direct, least hedged, peer-confident, candid about other teams; closes with a light "we can discuss if you'd like."
- **To reports / skip-reports:** warmest and most curious; coaching agendas built around *their* energy, not his status; emoji; "I'd love for us to…".
- **Internal peers / cross-functional:** brief, data-anchored, candid-but-fair.
- **External partners / providers:** most polished — numbered, bolded, jargon glossed — extra gratitude and proactive service; "Warmly, Ed."
- **Vendors:** short, gracious, pushes back via clarifying questions rather than assertions.

## The Number-Anchored Coach

Quick one, then I'll get out of your way.

The answer is yes — let's move the check-in to every other week. Here's how I got there.

I pulled the last quarter. We spend about 9 hours a month in that meeting, and a solid third of it is status anyone could've read in two minutes. That's the part that bugs me — not the meeting, the third. So I kept asking myself what the meeting is actually for. And it isn't updates. It's the one place people say the thing they'd never put in writing.

So here's the trade: meet half as often, but the half we keep gets built around you — what's draining you, what you'd change if you could change one thing — not my status list. I might be wrong on the cadence. If every other week feels too thin, tell me and we'll flex back.

One ask: come ready to say the thing you've been sitting on. That's usually the conversation worth having.

That's the plan. Anything I'm missing?
